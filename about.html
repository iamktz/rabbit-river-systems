I’m Kevin Butler. Rabbit River Systems is a new chapter built on a long working life in technology and sales.

I started in Navy electronics, taught technical courses, repaired systems in the field, and worked as a systems engineer. Later came more than twenty-five years in technology sales, including HP and Lenovo.

That combination matters to me. A solution has to make sense to the person choosing it, the people using it, and the business paying for it.

Understand what depends on the decision.

Some of my most satisfying work was with companies building their own products around HP technology. I worked with executives, technical teams, and operations staff to get the right pieces in place.

These weren’t abstract specifications. The hardware was part of what those businesses sold and supported. Getting it right meant understanding how they worked, what their customers needed, and what would happen if something failed.

Putting the customer first includes being candid.

I’ll ask questions before recommending something. I’ll explain a concern even when it makes the conversation harder. And I’d rather tell you a purchase isn’t necessary than find a reason to sell it.

The standard is straightforward: useful work, an agreed scope, and a commitment I can stand behind.

Why Rabbit River?

There was a little drainage crossing near our old home. Most days it was dry. When the rain came, the water moved fast. I called it Rabbit River when we drove past with the kids: small, but quick when it ran.

The name stayed with me. Today it fits a small business using experience and modern tools to make useful things—documents, diagnostics, and small applications with a specific job.

Nimble. Wise. Fruitful.

That’s the rabbit, and it took me longer to notice than the river did.

Nimble is the obvious one. Small, quick, able to change direction without calling a meeting about it.

Fruitful is the other obvious one, and slightly indelicate, which is why I like it. Rabbits produce. So should a business this size. Ideas are cheap and finished things are not, and the distance between the two is most of the work.

Wise is the one people miss. A rabbit that is only fast is dead by Tuesday. What actually keeps a rabbit alive is knowing when not to move: sitting still when stillness is the better play, and having the bolt-hole picked out well before there is any reason to run. Speed with no judgment about when to use it isn’t an advantage. It’s just noise with fur on it.

So the order matters. Wise gates nimble. Bad ideas published quickly and iterated quickly do not turn into good ideas. They turn into more bad ideas, and then into a folder full of half-finished ones. I know this because I have the folder.

In practice that looks unglamorous. Publish quickly, promote slowly. Finish the current thing before starting the interesting new thing. Let something earn its place before it gets a place.

Small by design.

I’m based in the Pacific Northwest. I’m building Rabbit River around focused work I can deliver personally, with room to keep learning and making.
