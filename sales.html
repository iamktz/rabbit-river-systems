Most sales aren't lost.
They're mismanaged.

Losing means somebody chose against you. Most of the time, nobody did.

The word is doing damage

"Lost" is a verdict word. It implies a jury. They heard both sides, weighed it, and picked the other guy. Sometimes that is exactly what happened. You were more expensive. You were slower. They had a brother-in-law.

Now go back through the ones from last quarter you would file under lost. How many of them actually went to a jury? How many ended with a person telling you no?

Most of them didn't end. They stopped. There is a difference, and the difference is worth money.

What mismanaged actually means

It isn't an insult, and it isn't about effort. You are not lazy. You are busy, which is a different problem and a worse one, because busy feels like working.

Mismanaged means the opportunity was real and nothing was managing it. A managed opportunity has three things: somebody knows it exists, it has a next step, and the next step has a date.

That's the whole definition. Miss any one of the three and the thing isn't being managed, it's being remembered. Memory is not a system. Memory is a guy in a truck at 6:40 at night trying to recall whether he ever heard back about the Carlson quote.

What it looks like

You quote a deck rebuild. Fourteen thousand, sent Thursday afternoon. He calls Friday with two good questions, you answer both, and he says he wants to talk it over with his wife. Reasonable. You write "Carlson, follow up" in the notebook on the dash.

The following week has a warranty callback in it, and a crew guy quits, and by Wednesday the notebook has four pages on top of that one.

In October you drive past the house and there is a new deck on it. Not your work. Nobody ever said no to you. Nobody said anything at all, which is the part that should bother you, because that means the job was still available the whole time and you were the only one who stopped showing up.

Why you never see it happen

A mismanaged sale does not complain. That is its defining feature. An unhappy customer calls you, and as unpleasant as that is, at least the information arrives.

Nobody calls to tell you that you were slow. Nobody writes to say your quote sat unopened, or that they went with the company that answered on a Tuesday morning while you were on a roof. The feedback loop is cut, so the only signal you ever get is a quieter month, and a quieter month gets blamed on the market.

It usually isn't the market. The market is just the last explanation standing after the real ones went unrecorded.

There are about seven places it happens

Not fifty. Seven, give or take, and they are ordinary. Slow first response. Quoting somebody who was never going to buy. Hearing the first thing a customer says and deciding you have heard enough. Sending a quote with no planned second touch. Never actually asking for the business.

I wrote each of them up, because owners who find their own on a page tend to recognize it faster than owners who are told which one it is.

What managing it takes

Less than people expect, and not what they usually buy first.

  • A list that lives outside your head. One place. Not texts, a notebook, email, and a good memory, which is four places and therefore zero.
  • A next step and a date on every open item. If you can't name the next step, the item is not open, it's decoration.
  • Permission to close things out as no. Dead opportunities left open are worse than lost ones, because they make the list untrustworthy, and an untrustworthy list gets ignored inside a month.

Software comes after that, not before. A tool laid over a process nobody has decided on will give you the same confusion at a higher speed and a monthly fee. I am building something small for the third part of this. It is not for sale, and I will not pretend otherwise until it is.

The honest limit

Managing your pipeline properly will not win you every job. You will still lose to price, to timing, and to the brother-in-law. Those are real losses and they are fine. That is what competing is.

What it stops is the other kind. The ones nobody decided against you on. Those are the expensive ones, and they are the only ones entirely inside your control.

It doesn't happen all at once. It happens in seven ordinary places.
