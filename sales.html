01 / Does this sound familiar?

Sales matters.
So does everything else.

Customers need answers. Jobs need finishing. Someone needs a decision. The work around selling consumes your time and attention before you get to the actual selling.

“I’m too busy to sell.”

A quote goes quiet. A follow-up slips. The day fills up, and sales gets pushed to tomorrow.

“Where do I start?”

Time finally opens up. Who should you contact? What happened last time? What did you promise? What should you say?

“Referrals keep us busy.”

That is a strong position. A practiced way to find new business adds resilience when a referral source, customer, or market changes.

02 / The 30-Minute Sales System

Use focused 30-minute blocks for the actual activity of selling, not preparing to sell.

Thirty minutes is a repeatable unit, not a daily ceiling. Schedule a few blocks a week, one a day, or several a day according to what your business needs.

  1. Before the block

    Prepare →

    Choose who needs attention. Gather context, prior conversations, and promises. Prepare the next useful action.

  2. During the block

    Sell →

    Call, email, text, follow up. Ask questions, qualify, and advance. Ask for the order when it is time.

  3. After each interaction

    Capture →

    Record what changed, who owns the next commitment, and when it happens. Update the opportunity’s state.

  4. For the next block

    Prepare ↺

    Use what you captured to get the next action ready. Repeat the rhythm before the week fills up.

AI prepares. Humans sell.

AI can help research, organize, prioritize, summarize, draft, and capture. You check the context, listen, exercise judgment, build trust, and make commitments.

06 / The selling still matters

A useful rhythm.
Sound sales judgment.

From a straightforward service quote to a complex solution sale, the principles hold. The depth of discovery, stakeholders, risk, and time change.

Always Be Prospecting.

Keep opportunities coming through referrals, relationships, and ordinary business conversations, outside scheduled selling blocks too.

Prospecting is sorting.

Is this person a prospect? Yes: discover. Not now: set a real date or trigger. No: qualify out cleanly and move on.

Don’t check in. Be ready with the next thing.

Bring the answer, revised estimate, or next useful question. Agree on what happens next, who owns it, and when.

Ask for the order.

Do not close before discovery is complete. Do not keep discovering after it is time to close.

Consistency beats blitzes.

Schedule enough focused blocks to meet the business need. Keep returning to the work instead of waiting for a giant sales day.

The thousand-pound phone

Get ready. Then make the call.

Fear of rejection and hesitation do not disappear because the notes are organized. Preparation reduces anxiety. A fuller funnel takes pressure off any single prospect. Eventually, you still have to call, send the message, ask the question, or ask for the order.

Rabbit River’s founder has spent decades in sales and technology, including enterprise environments with CRM, forecast calls, spreadsheets, and informal systems. Sales is messy at every scale. Preparation and clear next steps still matter.
