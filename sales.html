Sales for People
Who Hate Selling

You have a business to run. Selling comes with the territory. Becoming somebody else doesn’t.

If selling means pressure, a rehearsed pitch, or chasing people who don’t want to hear from you, no wonder it gets put off. A more useful starting point: find someone you may be able to help, ask a good question, and decide whether another step makes sense.

The thousand-pound telephone

Get ready. Then make the call.

Prospecting is sorting: finding out whether there is a useful fit before trying to sell a solution. Preparation should help you start that conversation.

Preparation can make the next step clearer. It cannot take the step for you.

Rabbit River works on the friction around that moment: who to contact, why them, what happened last time, and what to ask. The aim is to spend less of your selling time getting ready to sell, and more of it talking with customers.

Illustration of the direction · fictional example

You have 20 minutes.

A job wraps up early. Before the next appointment, you have time to follow up on an estimate. Here is the preparation that would help.

Ready before you reach out
Who to contact
Alex Morgan, a homeowner who requested a kitchen estimate.
Why today
You agreed to check in on Thursday after Alex reviewed the estimate.
Last conversation
Alex wanted to compare the repair and replacement options. Timing mattered; the preferred option was still undecided.
The next question
“After looking at the two options, what feels like the right direction, and what do you still need to decide?”
Your selling time

Check the context.
Make the call.

Listen for what has changed. Clarify fit and timing. If there is a useful next step, ask for it and agree on a date.

If Alex does not answer, leave a brief follow-up consistent with what you agreed. Record the result so the next selling session starts with context.

An unanswered call is an outcome to record, not evidence that the sale is lost.

This illustrates a workflow we are developing. It is not a customer result or an available software product.
