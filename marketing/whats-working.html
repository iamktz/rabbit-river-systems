← Back to MarketingIs My Marketing Actually Working?

Know What Deserves More, Less, or Nothing.

Look at time, cost, and useful results. Decide what to keep, fix, test, or stop.

The problem

Busy marketing is not the same as useful marketing.

You may already be posting, paying for ads, sending email, or working with someone on SEO. The question is whether anything useful changed, and whether the effort deserves to continue.

What you put in
List channels, campaigns, and outside help. Include subscription costs, spend, and your team’s time.
What customers do
Look at qualified inquiries, booked appointments, quote requests, customers, repeat business, referrals, and what people say brought them to you.
What the signals mean
Search visibility, helpful website behavior, and reviews can explain progress. Likes, followers, impressions, and raw traffic are not sufficient outcomes.
Marketing Reality Check · Available by request

Keep. Fix. Test. Stop.

Agree a period that fits how your customers buy and a manageable set of activities. Bring existing reports, costs, and what customers say. If the records are patchy, we start with that rather than requiring a new analytics system.

  1. Keep

    Continue work with a credible contribution to the outcomes that matter.

  2. Fix

    Improve a promising activity where the message, proof, page, or next step gets in the way.

  3. Test

    Give an uncertain idea a bounded budget, useful measure, and review date.

  4. Stop

    Retire work that cannot justify its cost or purpose after a fair assessment. Allow for the buying cycle and any deliberate work on awareness or trust.

Take away a Keep, Fix, Test, or Stop decision for each activity reviewed, with the evidence, next action, and review point. Where the evidence is too thin, the next step is to learn, not pretend to know.

Attribution honesty

The last visible step is not the whole story.

Illustrative example

A customer calls after searching your name.

They may first have seen a social post, encountered an AI answer, read reviews, and visited twice. Analytics may credit only the last observable step. Ask what influenced the choice and compare it with the data you have.

Start with three signals: what customers say brought them to you, a meaningful action such as an appointment or quote request, and the money and time invested. Review them consistently over an appropriate buying cycle. Use visibility and engagement to explain the picture, not replace it.
