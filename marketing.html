Start with the customer’s decision

Give people a reason
to choose your business.

More attention is useful when the right person can see what you offer, why it fits, and what to do next. Start with those answers before spending more on promotion.

Make the offer clear.

Explain whom you help, what you do, where you work, and what a customer can expect. Make the boundaries and pricing factors understandable.

Show what makes you different.

Use specific experience, genuine reviews, and useful explanations. Give customers evidence they can assess, rather than another promise of quality service.

Make the next step easy.

Help a visitor reach the right person, ask a question, or request the right service. Follow through when interest becomes an inquiry.

AI recommendation readiness

When customers ask AI who to call,
does it know your business?

A prospective customer may ask an AI system to research providers before visiting a website. What could it learn about your business—and what important answers would it be missing?

You may hear this called answer engine optimization, or AEO. The useful starting point is practical: accurate business information, real expertise, clear evidence, and an easy way to act.

Start with what’s there.

Review your website, business listings, and other public information. Look for missing services, conflicting details, stale information, and unclear claims.

Use what your business knows.

Capture the questions your customers actually ask. Turn your team’s answers into useful explanations of the work, the choices, and the tradeoffs.

Connect interest to action.

Make service areas, fit, contact details, and next steps easy to find. Check what happens after someone reaches out.

No one can promise that an AI system will recommend your business. Clearer information gives customers better material to make a decision, regardless of which tool they use to research it.

What do customers ask that your website never answers?

Your business may already have the raw material: questions from sales calls, explanations from your team, recurring objections, and details that change the price or the right solution.

Compare those real questions with what you have published. Keep private customer information private; publish the useful answer, with the business’s review and permission.

Illustrative example · not a customer result

“Can you clean our gutters without disrupting the business?”

A commercial customer may need to know about access, working hours, cleanup, and how the job affects employees or visitors. “Reliable gutter cleaning” leaves those questions unanswered.

A useful service page explains how the company handles occupied premises, what needs checking first, and how to request an estimate. That gives both the customer and a research tool more specific information to work with.

Make your knowledge useful in more than one place.

A well-supported answer can improve a service page, a sales follow-up, a short guide, or an internal reference. Start with information your business can stand behind. Choose where to use it based on how customers actually find and evaluate you.

Measure attention and what happens next.

Agree on what would count as improvement before doing the work. Depending on the problem, that might include relevant inquiries, response time, booked conversations, qualified opportunities, or sales.

Track visibility where reliable reporting is available, and ask customers how they found you. A few sample AI answers can reveal gaps; they cannot establish a dependable ranking or prove what caused a sale.
