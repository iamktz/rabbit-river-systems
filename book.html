Twenty minutes.

No pitch. You'll leave with one specific thing to look at — whether or not you ever hire me.

What actually happens on the call

You tell me roughly how work comes in and what happens to it after that. I ask a handful of questions. Between us we figure out which of the seven is most likely costing you, and I tell you the smallest thing I'd do about it.

That's it. If the answer is something you can go do yourself in an afternoon, I'll say so, and that will be the whole call. I'd rather be useful in twenty minutes than be vague for an hour.

What I'll ask you to have ready

  • A rough sense of how many quotes or estimates you send in a month.
  • What happens right now when somebody contacts you and you can't answer.
  • One recent job that went quiet, if you can think of one.

Nothing to prepare, nothing to send me in advance, and I don't need access to anything of yours.

What this isn't

  • Not a discovery call that turns into a proposal you didn't ask for.
  • Not an AI pitch. Sometimes the answer involves software and often it doesn't.
  • Not the start of a retainer conversation unless you bring it up.
