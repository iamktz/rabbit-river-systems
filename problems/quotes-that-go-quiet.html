Quotes that go quiet

A quote needs a next step. Agree on when to follow up, find out what is holding up the decision, and keep the conversation useful.

← Back to sales guides

What's actually happening

The quote goes out and the process ends. There's no default next step, because follow-up depends on you remembering, and you are on a job site with your phone in a truck.

So "they went quiet" became a normal outcome instead of an alarm. Nobody said no. They just stopped, and you never found out why, and after a while you stopped expecting to.

What it costs you

This is the most expensive item on this page and the easiest to fix, which is a maddening combination.

You already did the work. You looked at it, measured it, thought about it, priced it, wrote it up. All of that is spent. Walking away at that point isn't losing a sale; it's donating one.

What it looks like

Try this right now: name every quote you sent in the last month and what happened to each one.

Most owners can name the wins, one or two of the losses, and then it goes fuzzy. The fuzzy ones are the money. Not because they said no, because nobody ever asked them again.

Where I'd start

One second touch, scheduled, that happens whether or not you remember.

  • A specific day when you'll check in, decided the moment you send the quote, not "in a few days."
  • A message you don't have to compose each time. Two sentences. Reusable.
  • A third and final touch that gives them an easy way to say no. A clean no is worth more than a maybe you carry for six months.

Most of what looks like lost work is just unfinished work.

Not sure if this is the one costing you?

That's the usual situation, and it's worth twenty minutes. Most owners guess wrong about which of the seven is theirs; the loudest problem is almost never the expensive one.

Book 20 minutes

Twenty minutes, phone or video. What's happening, and what I'd look at first.
