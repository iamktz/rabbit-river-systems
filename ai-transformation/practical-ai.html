Use AI Where It Earns Its Place.

Start with recurring work and test whether AI improves the result enough to justify the effort.

The problem

“We should use AI” is not a useful job description.

Start with recurring friction, not with the technology. Where are people repeatedly losing time, clarity, courage, or momentum? Then ask whether assistance would improve that specific work.

Prepare and understand
Research, sales preparation, analysis, summaries, and internal knowledge retrieval can be candidates when the sources and review criteria are clear.
Draft and organize
Marketing support, document processing, customer-service drafts, and repetitive administration may benefit from a useful first pass.
Keep human judgment visible
Review facts, suitability, commitments, and sensitive information. A faster draft is only useful if the finished result is good enough.
AI Workflow Opportunity Review · Available by request

Choose one recurring task with a visible result.

  1. Describe today’s job

    Capture the inputs, steps, owner, expected result, and common failures.

  2. Check the fit

    Consider a process change, existing automation, or AI assistance. Use only the information and permissions the job needs.

  3. Test a small version

    Use representative examples, including awkward cases. Keep consequential actions reviewable.

  4. Count the whole effort

    Include preparation, corrections, review, and maintenance. Keep the approach only if the result justifies the effort.

A useful first experiment

Draft the summary. Keep the commitment with a person.

Illustrative example

An inquiry becomes a prepared handoff.

A tool summarizes an incoming request and lists missing facts. The responsible person checks the summary, asks the follow-up questions, and makes any promise to the customer. Compare handling time and correction effort with the current process.
