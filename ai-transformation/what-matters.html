Know What Actually Matters.

Separate material changes from vendor noise and decide what deserves attention now.

The problem

Change is constant. Attention is limited.

Rapid AI change, software churn, vendor pressure, and news about competitors can make every decision feel urgent. The question is whether the change materially affects the work your business needs to do.

The business consequence
What customer experience, operating constraint, cost, or risk could change? Be specific about who would notice.
The evidence
Distinguish a demonstrated capability from a vendor promise. Look at what would have to be true for it to work in your setting.
The timing
Is there a real deadline, dependency, or cost of waiting? Competitor adoption alone does not establish the right timing for you.
Technology Priorities Brief · Available by request

A plain-language view of what deserves attention.

A bounded review can examine one technology decision or a short set of options. Depending on the question, the output might be a readiness review, an AI opportunity brief, or a small practical roadmap.

Use what already works
Check whether an existing tool or process change can address the need.
Run a small test
Name the expected improvement, the cost of testing, the review point, and what would cause you to stop.
Defer with a reason
Record the uncertainty or missing dependency. Revisit when a specific condition changes.
A decision you can explain

Separate interest from a reason to act.

Illustrative example

“Everyone is adding an AI assistant.”

That is a reason to look, not a buying case. If staff repeatedly search scattered documents, test whether an assistant can answer a defined set of questions accurately from approved information. Include review time in the comparison.
