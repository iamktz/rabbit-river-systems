← Back to Tech & AIWhy do we have so many tools?

Simplify the Stack.

Reduce duplicate subscriptions, scattered information, and workflows built around the software.

The problem

The tools keep multiplying. The work does not get simpler.

Every tool may have made sense when it arrived. Together, they can create duplicate work, avoidable expense, and uncertainty about where the real record lives.

Overlapping subscriptions
Several products cover similar jobs, but no one is sure which features are used or which account is still needed.
Fragmented information
Too many logins, scattered files, and separate records make people search, copy, and reconcile the same facts.
A workflow built around the software
Staff maintain workarounds and unused features instead of following a clear path from request to result.
Tool Stack Reality Check · Available by request

Keep what works. Remove what does not. Connect what should.

Start with one team or workflow and a bounded inventory: tool, owner, purpose, users, cost, important records, and connections. The review should leave you with specific keep, consolidate, connect, or retire decisions.

Check actual use
Ask what people depend on, not just what a product can theoretically do. Include spreadsheets and informal workarounds.
Check dependencies
Before retiring a tool, identify records to preserve, connections that rely on it, and people affected by the change.
Make a manageable change
Choose an owner and a transition plan. Test the new path before removing something the business still needs.
A bounded review

One workflow before a whole-business inventory.

Begin with something recognizable, such as how a customer request becomes a scheduled job. Identify each place information is entered, copied, or lost. A simpler handoff may matter more than replacing every application.
