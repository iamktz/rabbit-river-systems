Remove Repetitive Work.

Find a simpler way to prepare reports, move files, clean up information, or repeat the same administrative steps.

The problem

The same small job keeps interrupting the day.

Someone prepares the same report, moves files, retypes details, cleans up another spreadsheet, or chases a routine handoff. Each step may look minor until it repeats across the week.

Describe one complete job
What starts it, what information is needed, what should happen, and who needs the result? Include the awkward cases.
Measure the repeated effort
Note frequency, people involved, time, errors, and delays. Separate a recurring problem from a rare annoyance.
Remove the unnecessary step
A shared template, a clearer process, or a built-in feature may resolve the friction before anything is automated.
Integration / Workflow Friction Scan · Available by request

Choose the smallest intervention that removes the work.

The scan examines an agreed workflow and its handoffs. You receive a map of repeated effort, prioritized improvements, and a recommended first test with an owner and a measure of success.

  1. Use what exists

    Check settings, templates, existing features, and supported connections.

  2. Automate predictable steps

    Use rules when the inputs and actions are stable. Make failures visible and keep a recovery path.

  3. Add assistance where needed

    Use AI for interpreting variable material when its output can be checked. Keep important decisions with a person.

  4. Build only when justified

    Consider a small utility when simpler options cannot resolve proven recurring friction. Agree who will maintain it.

Repeated friction earns automation. Repeated proven value earns custom software.

Before a build

Prove the friction, then prove the value.

Do not build internal software until repeated friction is proven, unless the smallest prototype is the cheapest way to prove whether the friction is worth solving. A prototype is a test, not an automatic commitment to a permanent system.

Illustrative example

A weekly report uses four predictable files.

Start with an existing report feature or a repeatable import and template. Test missing files and duplicate rows, and keep a person responsible for the finished report. This may need a small automation, not an agent.

Do not automate a process you cannot describe, observe, and judge. Implementation is separately scoped; no ready-made automation is included simply by choosing this page.
