For owner-led service businesses

Most sales aren't lost. They're mismanaged.

Not by careless people. By busy ones. Somewhere between the first call and the final follow-up, work you already earned quietly slips away — and it almost never looks like a lost sale. It looks like a quote nobody ever answered.

It starts earlier than most owners think: with what a stranger — or an AI assistant — finds when they go looking for you.

Field note

Most engagements start with one question: what actually happens after somebody asks you for an estimate? Almost nobody knows the honest answer.

01Recognition

Mismanaged doesn't mean careless. It means unowned.

Sales don't vanish all at once. They leak at five predictable points. Most businesses are solid at four of them and quietly bleeding at one — and it's rarely the one they'd guess.

  • 00

    Representation New

    You can't manage what you can't see — and right now you probably can't see what a machine is telling people about you. Your hours, your service area, whether you're even mentioned when somebody asks an assistant for a recommendation.

  • 01

    Prospecting

    You're spending your best hours on people who were never going to buy. Every call gets treated the same, so the serious ones wait while you write a careful quote for somebody who was only curious.

  • 02

    Roles

    Everybody sells a little. Nobody owns it. Whoever's closest answers the phone, follow-up happens if someone remembers, and the owner is the sales department, the estimator and half the crew.

  • 03

    Pipeline

    You can't see what's actually open. Quotes live in your texts, your email and your head — and nothing tells you which ones have gone quiet.

  • 04

    Closing

    The quote went out. Then nothing. There's no second touch unless the customer calls, and "they went quiet" has become a normal outcome. Nobody said no — they just stopped.

None of that is a demand problem. It's a handling problem — and handling problems are fixable, usually without spending another dollar on leads.

Stage 00 · The Front Door Check

Is your business ready for agentic search?

Search changed. People still Google you — but increasingly they ask an assistant instead, and the assistant answers on their behalf. It can only repeat what it finds.

This isn't a scan you wait on. It's six things you can check yourself, right now, in about two minutes. Nothing to install, no account, and I don't need access to anything of yours.

Check 01

Ask an assistant to recommend somebody like you.

Open ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot and ask: "Recommend a [your trade] near [your town]."

Check 02

Search your own business name.

Is the top result yours, current, and correct?

Check 03

Do your details agree with each other?

Hours, phone and service area — on your website, your map listing, and anywhere else you're listed.

Check 04

The five-second test.

Open your own homepage on a phone. Could a stranger tell what you do and where you work, in five seconds?

Check 05

Try to contact yourself.

From that same phone, one-handed: how many taps to reach a human or ask for an estimate?

Check 06

Read your three most recent reviews.

Did anybody reply to them?

Nothing is sent anywhere. This runs in your browser.
