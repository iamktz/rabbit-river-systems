Know What’s Working and What Isn’t.

See what helps you sell, where effort is wasted, and which problem to fix first.

The problem

Plenty of activity.
Too little clarity.

You spend time and money on a website, leads, quotes, outreach, advertising, referrals, and follow-up. It can still be hard to tell which efforts create real conversations and which opportunities quietly disappear.

Where do prospects enter?
Look at the source of inquiries and which activities lead to useful conversations, rather than counting activity alone.
Where do they stall?
Find inquiries waiting for a response, quotes going stale, weak follow-up, unclear next steps, and handoffs without an owner.
What deserves attention first?
Separate an observed breakdown from a guess. Choose one practical correction and decide what would show that it helped.
One problem, a focused set of tools · In development

Find the leak. Make a bounded fix.

Sales Leak Finder
Use a small set of inquiries, quotes, and follow-up records to identify likely breakdowns in the sales path.
Smart Sales Scan
Inspect the public-facing path for visible friction in message, trust, response options, and calls to action. Verify the internal impact with the owner.
Fix Kits
Route a diagnosed problem to a focused correction, such as clearer next steps, a response routine, or a quote follow-up checklist.

These tools and kits are in development. The aim is a useful diagnosis and a specific correction, not another reporting dashboard.

What to bring

A small, representative slice is enough.

Start with recent inquiries, quotes, where they came from, what happened, and the last or next action. A spreadsheet or a short written list can work. Include what you know and mark what is missing.

What a useful diagnosis should leave you with

One priority you can act on.

For example: if inquiries repeatedly wait without an owner, agree who responds and by when. Then look at whether those inquiries receive a response and lead to a conversation. The correction should address the actual bottleneck.
