Fill the Funnel.

Create a steadier flow of likely-fit prospects and arrive with a useful reason to talk.

The problem

Growth needs a steady supply of conversations.

Prospecting gets pushed aside while you deliver today’s work. Then the funnel gets thin, and finding new business becomes a panicked burst.

Always Be Prospecting.

Keep referrals, relationships, and new contacts moving even when you are busy. Prospecting continues outside scheduled selling blocks, too.

Prospecting is sorting.

The first contact answers a simple question: does this person belong in the prospect bucket? Learn whether another conversation makes sense.

The working solution · In development

Rabbit River Fill the Funnel Kit

A toolset we are developing to help you find likely-fit businesses, inspect their public presence, prepare the contact, and move promising prospects into the selling queue. Rabbit River will use it as Customer Zero.

  1. Agentic Prospect Search

    Find →

    Use geography, industry, observable business characteristics, public signals, and your fit criteria to identify businesses worth a look.

  2. Smart Sales Scan

    Inspect →

    Look for strengths, visible friction, and useful conversation starters. Public observations are clues to verify, not proof of an internal problem.

  3. Prospect Prep

    Prepare →

    Bring together why this prospect, relevant facts, observations, a likely opener, a few useful questions, and the desired next step.

  4. Ready queue

    Sell

    Move promising prospects into a focused selling block. Make the contact, listen, sort, and agree on the next step.

Software elements are in development. Pilot scope and availability are discussed individually.

First contact

Three useful outcomes.

A first conversation does not have to end in an order to be worthwhile.

Yes, interested
Move into discovery. Understand the issue before suggesting a solution.
Interested, but not now
Agree on a real future date or trigger and record why you will reconnect.
No
Qualify out cleanly. Record the answer and put your attention where there may be a fit.
A practical starting point

Define who is worth talking to.

Start with an area, a kind of business, and a reason your work may matter to them. Prepare enough to make contact intelligently, then learn from the conversation. Consistency beats blitzes.

Who would be a good next customer?

Bring the kind of business you want to reach. We can discuss the fit criteria, useful preparation, and whether a future pilot makes sense.
